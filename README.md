# Pikku Norppa

A little ringed seal to look after, on a frozen lake in Finnish Lapland. A 3D virtual pet that runs in the browser and works well on a phone.

**Play:** https://detailedfoil.github.io/Wife-/

On a phone, open the link and tap **Add to Home Screen** on the first screen to get it as an app with its own icon (Chrome, Edge and Samsung Internet open the browser's own install prompt; iPhone, iPad, Firefox and others show the steps instead). After the first visit it also opens with no signal.

## What is in it

- **A seal that grows.** A white pup (kuutti) becomes a silver youngster and then a ringed grown-up, by friendship level, not by age: a youngster at level 6 and a grown-up at level 10, however many days old, so a level-10 seal is old enough to have pups. A grown seal who has earned 1,009 friendship xp (what it takes to reach level 10) and is awake, fed, happy and not sulking may have a pup of her own; seals already at level 10 who never had one are owed one the next time the game loads. Once she has the xp nothing happens while you play: close the game, and the next time it opens she has met a special someone while you were away, with a message to come back in 9 hours, which is when the pup is born and appears (you are told when you open the game after that). A birth is one pup, or twins about one time in seven; pups are grown and ready to swim off after 2 days, and the 1,009 xp for the next birth only starts counting once the last ones have left.
- **Fair play.** A mini-game gives a point of friendship for every 3 seconds played, up to 8, and its happiness change in proportion, so ending a game at once on purpose no longer counts as a full game.
- **Looking after it.** Feed, stroke, take her to the sauna, tuck in (the friendship for it comes once she has slept half an hour), clear up after it. Four needs: tummy, happiness, energy, cleanliness.
- **Time passes while you are away.** The game saves when you leave and works out what happened when you come back, so a seal left alone for a day really has had a day alone. It sleeps at night by your own clock.
- **It needs looking after about five or six times a day, but the seal never dies.** Hunger drops to a quarter in about six hours and hits empty in about eight. Needs run at the same pace whether the game is open or closed. A seal left hungry or grubby loses friendship points (happiness no longer counts as neglect: a glum seal just earns no happiness bonus) and stops growing, then slips back, and after a while falls poorly, but she no longer gets angry from being left alone. Angry now comes only from a bad dive through the ice hole (and anxiety from a late medicine); a four hour nap clears a full angry spell, and each of the three treats she asks for takes a fifth off it, as do the head pats together and a sauna. Saves that were angry from neglect are cleared once. Once a day keeps it alive but it will not grow up; five or six visits a day keeps it thriving, like an old-style Tamagotchi. The mini-games get quicker the longer you last, and feeding is always allowed. A grown seal can have pups (two at most), each born wearing a little thing of its own (one in ten gets two) so the family can be told apart, and a pup whose mother goes hungry for a couple of days can be lost. The Family album shows a little portrait of every pup in its own outfit, and a pup that has grown up can be called home from there: it arrives an hour later (bringing 100 coins, but only the first two visits of each day bring a present), stays on the shore until you send it home or 8 hours are up, and can be called again 12 hours after the call; two visitors at a time at most. Pop-ups ignore taps for the first half second so a double tap cannot choose Close by accident. Waving a grown pup goodbye pays 200 coins. Developer mode has pup tools in Settings: a new pup now, pups ready to leave, farewell now, new outfits, poorly pups and feeding them back to health. When money runs out, the neighbours spare fish, and juice for a poorly seal.
- **Seal Seat Shuffle (100, the cheapest game to buy).** Seats score from the worst free seat (0) to the best (100), and every bus waits its full time, so a seat tapped without looking pays little; the old bests were cleared once when this changed. A tall yellow-and-blue roofless Three.js bus, with an open windscreen and the game's seal models. Twenty buses, 1.1 seconds per choice, and up to 2,000 points. Each bus arrives fast, eases into a visibly moving continuous crawl during the choice and heat-map reveal, with framing that allows the full travel, then accelerates away. There is no countdown display; the choice deadline still starts when the crawl begins. Both length and width increase every four buses: eight to sixteen rows, and one to five seats on each side. Choose the seat furthest from its nearest passenger. Every tap selects the closest empty seat by its current on-screen position, including near misses in the aisle, on the road, or on an occupied seat while the bus moves. Passenger placement varies the open gap between corners, edges and the interior, checking real nearest-passenger distances before accepting a layout; corner, edge and interior scores follow the same rule. In a seeded simulation of 10,000 full games before and after (200,000 buses per version), at least one corner was optimal on 50.7% of old buses versus 12.1% with the varied layouts. Generation has bounded retries for phones. After each choice, all empty seats show a heat map from red (worst) through yellow to green (best); a white ring identifies your chosen seat. Timeouts score zero. The lower perspective view fits portrait phones; scores, coins and personal bests use the existing game system.
- **A live sky.** The sun and moon are worked out for Inari in Finnish Lapland (69 degrees north) at this very moment, wherever you are, so it gets dark when it gets dark there: polar night in midwinter, midnight sun in midsummer. The temperature, cloud, wind and snowfall are the real ones over Inari, fetched from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/) every quarter of an hour, with a made-up forecast as the fallback when there is no signal. Northern lights show on clear dark nights.

Every game was simulated at four skill levels and pays about the same per minute whichever you pick: roughly 16 coins a minute for a beginner, 24 casual, 31 good and 37 expert. Every game then pays 7.5% on top of its curve (the 150 cap becomes about 161), and a game costs half the energy it used to: about one point for every seven seconds played, at least 2 and at most 20, and she needs 6 energy to play. The sauna replaces the bath and is how she gets clean: the dock's Sauna button (or a tap on the sauna on the shore) plays Löyly, a game like Stardew fishing where you hold to keep the heat needle in the green. It is free, costs no energy and is not in the Play list. Each round scrubs a seventh of the dirt away, so seven rounds in one go is a full clean from filthy; a go asks for just as many rounds as it takes to finish her, whatever she managed counts, and she will not take one once she is 95% clean, unless she is angry and still wants her sauna. A go that finishes the job pays 12 coins (less in proportion if the heat gets away first), but only for a real wash, begun under 60% clean; a top-up pays nothing, and one that leaves her 60% clean or better counts as the bath she wants when she is angry. Patting is only a little cheer (a fifth of a point a pat, the first ten an hour, counted the same whether she is awake or asleep, and a cuddle for a pup cheers its mother out of the same ten) and so is the nose ball (a point a bounce, the first six an hour, and it costs no energy), so games she fancies are what lift her happiness. The one exception is coffee (Kahvi, 400 coins): one mug fills her tummy, happiness and energy at once, though not her cleanliness, and it cures nothing (angry, anxiety, poorly). She has to be awake to drink it, it is never one of the treats an angry seal asks for, and an evening mug does not keep her up once the game is closed. It is dear on purpose, for a visit with no time to play. The shop and the pantry show each thing's Finnish name with what it does underneath (Tummy +9, Energy +35, and so on), and shrimp dropped from 25 to 10 coins now that it is only a snack. Otherwise food and drink only fill her tummy (and 35 energy for the Sealsius, 50 coins a can), they no longer touch her happiness, favourite fish included. Care feeds into the games. Every game leaves her muckier (3 to 12 cleanliness points a minute by game, at most 15 a game), and at 50% clean or below she earns 1% less, climbing steeply as it falls (2% at 40, 5% at 30, 7% at 25, 16% at 10) to a quarter less when filthy. She also has tastes: each game she owns carries a taste from -15 to +15, and a game moves her happiness by 3.5 + 1.05 × taste points for a usual 45 second play (−12 to +19, 1.75 times the old 2 + 0.6 × taste), times a length factor of (seconds ÷ 45) to the power 0.85, at most 2.5, so a game that takes minutes moves her happiness more, up or down (Coffee Memory at about two minutes is 2.3 times; a 20 second go is a third of it); the Play menu shows each game's figure per minute of play (its figure for a usual play scaled to a minute, labelled "Per minute"), so a long game cannot look better just by being long, shown in the Play menu as a smiley or sad face, an arrow and the number. Her happiness sets one flat bonus on every game: +8% at full happiness, rising from nothing at 55, and never a penalty (the bonus goes by her happiness when a game begins). Games no longer pay more or less one by one. Playing a game takes 5 off its taste and shares 5 among the others, and tastes drift 2.5 points an hour towards a fresh random mix dealt every 8 hours. Up to two games can be left out of the mix; those are never favourites but still wear out. Fish and food cost a quarter more than before (a vendace is 8 coins) and things to wear or put on the shore 15% less (from 170 coins); the medicine is unchanged. In simulation, from a usual 70 happiness a player who keeps picking her favourites reaches the full +8% in about 4 games and holds it, while a random mix takes 10 to 18 games; nobody earns less than before. Puffer Sweeper is the exception, because it pays by the clock: 60.5 × seconds^-0.3 coins for a cleared board (30 for ten seconds or less, 25 for 20 seconds, 22 for 30, 18 for a minute, 14 for two) and nothing for a lost one, so quick players earn more a minute there and slow ones less. Playing tires her by how long the game lasted (about 12 energy for a 40 second game), not a flat amount per game. Also dives through the ice hole for fish and treasures, a daily gift and visit streak (an angry seal keeps the gift back and breaks the streak; the medicine mood does not, since the pill is due from 5 am and the first visit of the day would always find her anxiety). Growing up pays the 50 coins its pop-up shows.
- **Coffee Memory (850 coins, between Puffer Sweeper and Curling Seal).** A 3D mini-game in the Play list like the others, with a taste of its own and 3 cleanliness points a minute. It is seen through the seal's own eyes from behind the counter of a cosy log cafe. A customer (an animal of the Finnish woods: reindeer, bear, fox, hare, owl, moose, lynx, squirrel) walks up and their order comes as a receipt, which is held up to read for as long as you like and then turned face down. The drink is then built from memory, one step at a time, in the same order every time: cup size, base (bags of dark roast, light roast, decaf, matcha), hot or iced (a coffee machine or a blender of ice), milk (cartons, or a jug of water for none), syrup (bottles, or an empty one with a cross for none), a second syrup, extras, and the customer's name written on the cup. The things to choose from slide along the counter from the right and away to the left, and the cup visibly fills as it goes. The extras step moves on by itself once every ordered extra has been tapped; tapping one that was not ordered is a wrong drink. The first order is two things (size and base) and every second drink served adds one more, up to ten (so ten things from the seventeenth drink on): temperature, milk and syrup, then one extra, then the name as the seventh thing (before the second extra), then more extras one at a time (never all four, which would be too easy to remember), and the second syrup last (the name is typed; the names are English ones in an English game and Finnish ones with no accented letters in a Finnish game, and capitals do not matter). Customers keep coming for as long as every drink is right; the first wrong choice ends the game, and the receipt comes back marked with what was right, what was wrong and what you made. Pay is `0.35 × drinks^1.6` coins, so a long run pays far more per drink than a short one: 0.77 × drinks^1.85 coins, so 4, 6, 9, 11 and 14 drinks pay about 10, 21, 46, 65 and 100 coins (11 drinks in about two minutes is the other games' 31 coins a minute); the 150 cap is reached around 18 drinks. There is no clock. Every label is painted in the language in use. On a tall screen the choices stand in two rows, the back row on a step; on a wide one in a single row. It is kept out of her tastes so it never changes the real mix. The rules and the 3D cafe are both in `js/coffee.js` (the rules first, so they can be tested without a browser); the arcade fetches that file again if the phone is holding an old copy of it.

## Saving

The seal is kept in the browser's own storage on the device, so there is no account. Clearing the browser's site data clears the seal.

## Play stats

The game sends anonymous play stats (a random device id, which game, how long it was played, the score, the coins it paid, how the game changed her happiness and energy, the language, the coin balance, her needs and level when you open the game, games unlocked and things bought, plays that were blocked and why, the screen size, whether it is installed as an app, how smoothly games ran, which Coffee Memory step went wrong, and any script errors) to a Supabase table, so the maker can see which games are liked. No name, email or anything personal is sent, nothing is sent in developer mode, and **Share play stats** in Settings turns it off. Events wait on the phone when there is no signal and go later. The code is `norppa/js/track.js`; the private stats page is at `stats/` (it needs a passcode), and `stats/setup.sql` is the database setup. Settings has a save code for moving a seal to another phone or browser.

## How it is built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with [three.js](https://threejs.org) for the 3D. No build step: the files in this repository are exactly what runs. Every model is made from code, so there are no model, image or sound files to load.

```
index.html              sends visitors to norppa/
norppa/
  index.html            the page
  css/style.css         the interface
  js/data.js            fish, food, clothes, decorations, words, awards
  js/lang.js            the Finnish text
  js/install.js         the Add to Home Screen button (the install prompt, or the steps)
  js/sim.js             the rules: needs, time away, growing, pups, rewards, saving
  js/coffee.js          Coffee Memory: its rules (orders and checking), then its 3D cafe
  js/astro.js           sun, moon, season and weather for the date and time
  js/audio.js           sound effects and music
  js/props.js           every small 3D object
  js/seal.js            the seal: body, face, moods, moves
  js/fx.js              hearts, sparkles, bubbles, splashes
  js/world.js           the shore, lake, sky, lights and camera
  js/ui.js              bars, menus, messages
  js/games.js           Fish Rain, Nose Ball and the shared game screen
  js/main.js            ties it all together
  js/track.js           anonymous play stats sent to Supabase
  sw.js                 offline support
stats/
  index.html            private stats page (passcode)
  setup.sql             database tables, limits and the passcode check
  vendor/three.min.js   three.js r160 (MIT licence)
  fonts/                DynaPuff and Nunito (SIL Open Font Licence)
```
